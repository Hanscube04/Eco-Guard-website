Turning kitchen waste into compost: a starter guide

Home / Blog / Composting Guide
← Back to Blog

A surprising share of household waste is organic — food scraps, peels, and garden trimmings — and almost all of it can be turned into useful compost instead of landfill waste. Here's a simple way to start.

What you can compost

  • Fruit and vegetable peels or scraps
  • Eggshells
  • Coffee grounds and tea leaves
  • Dry leaves and small garden trimmings

What to avoid

  • Meat, bones, and dairy (they attract pests and smell)
  • Oily or heavily cooked food
  • Plastic or synthetic materials of any kind

The basic method

Layer your organic waste with dry material like dry leaves or shredded paper in a bin, pit, or simple pile. Turn it every few days to add air, and keep it slightly moist — not wet. Within a few weeks, it will start breaking down into dark, crumbly compost.

Using your compost

Once it's dark, crumbly, and smells earthy rather than rotten, it's ready. Mix it into garden soil or use it for potted plants — either way, waste that would have gone to a dumpsite is now feeding something new.

This is one of the practical skills we teach in every Eco-Club training session. If your school or household wants a hands-on walkthrough, reach out and we'll help you set it up.
