Five simple ways to segregate waste at home

Home / Blog / Waste Segregation
← Back to Blog

Most household waste never needs to reach a landfill in the first place. With a few small habits, families can cut down the amount of trash that leaves their home while making it easier to recycle or compost what's left. Here are five simple ways to get started.

1. Start with two bins, not ten

You don't need a complicated system to begin. One bin for wet, organic waste (food scraps, peels, leftovers) and one for dry waste (plastic, paper, glass, metal) is enough to make a real difference from day one.

2. Rinse before you toss

A quick rinse of containers and bottles keeps recyclables clean and prevents food residue from attracting pests or contaminating an entire bin of otherwise recyclable material.

3. Keep a small compost corner

Fruit peels, vegetable trimmings, and eggshells can go straight into a small compost bin or pit instead of the trash. Over a few weeks, they break down into nutrient-rich soil for a home garden.

4. Flatten and stack

Cardboard boxes and plastic bottles take up far less space when flattened. This makes storage easier at home and collection more efficient when waste collectors or Eco-Guard volunteers arrive.

5. Involve the whole household

Segregation only works if everyone participates. Label bins clearly, explain the "why" to children and other household members, and make it a shared routine rather than one person's job.

Small habits like these, repeated across many households, are exactly the kind of change Eco-Guard's waste management campaigns are built around. If your neighborhood wants support getting started, reach out — we're happy to help set up a segregation pilot.
